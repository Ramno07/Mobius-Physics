Why the name MOBIUS ?

" August Ferdinand Möbius "

was a German mathematician and astronomer best known for his important contributions to geometry, topology, number theory, and astronomy. He was born on 17 November 1790 in Schulpforta, Saxony, and died on 26 September 1868 in Leipzig, Germany.

Möbius was born into a family connected with education. His father, Johann Heinrich Möbius, was a dance teacher at the famous Schulpforta school, while his mother came from a scholarly family. At first, Möbius considered studying mathematics, but he was also interested in astronomy and physics. He entered the University of Leipzig in 1809, where he initially studied law before changing his field to mathematics, astronomy, and philosophy.

He later studied under the famous mathematician and astronomer Carl Friedrich Gauss at the University of Göttingen. Möbius also studied under Carl Friedrich von Wrede and developed a strong interest in astronomy. In 1815, he received his doctorate from the University of Leipzig.

Career in astronomy:
Möbius worked extensively as an astronomer. In 1816, he became an assistant at the observatory in Leipzig, and later became a professor of astronomy at the University of Leipzig. His astronomical work included celestial mechanics, the mathematical study of the motion of planets and other astronomical bodies. Although he is remembered today mainly for mathematics, astronomy was actually a major part of his professional career. Contributions to mathematics Möbius made important contributions to several areas of mathematics.

The Möbius Strip:
The Möbius strip is the mathematical object for which Möbius is most famous.
A Möbius strip can be made very simply using a long, narrow strip of paper.

How to make one:
1.Take a strip of paper.
2.Give one end a 180° half-twist.
3.Join the two ends together.
